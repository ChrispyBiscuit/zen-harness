# Web research and hard-to-find information

For questions about the real world — places, people, products, prices, news, anything that changes — the agent
searches the web and answers with its sources. It races several search engines at once, reads the best pages, and
checks figures and quotes against what it read.

## When normal search isn't enough

Some answers don't sit on the first page of results: an old page that was deleted, a figure buried in a PDF, a record
in a specialist database, a forum thread, a page in another language. When the agent's first few searches fail, it
switches to its **hard-target playbook** instead of just rewording the query. That method covers:

- **Defining the target** — what a correct answer looks like, so it knows when it has found it.
- **Going where the answer lives** — specialist indexes and databases (scholarly, legal and government records, code,
  books and newspapers, product data) instead of general search.
- **Search operators** that differ by engine (`site:`, `filetype:`, date limits) and which engines have independent
  indexes.
- **Archives** — the Wayback Machine and other archives for dead, changed or deleted pages; ways around blocked,
  paywalled or script-built pages where that is legitimate.
- **Digging inside one site** — sitemaps, a site's own search, its APIs, and structured sources such as Wikidata,
  Wikipedia and OpenStreetMap.
- **Verification** — confirming a find from independent sources, and knowing when to stop.

## Asking for it

You don't need special commands. These help:
- Say how hard you expect it to be: *"this is obscure — dig properly, try archives and specialist sources"*.
- Give anything you already know: a name, a date range, a place, a document number, a language.
- Say what counts as an answer: *"I need the original source, not a summary"*.

The agent keeps a short log of the routes it tried in its checklist, so you can see what it searched and why it
moved on. Searches go to public search engines and websites — see [Your data and privacy](data-and-privacy.md).
